\section{The long run}\label{sec:sp:longrun}

This section characterises the long-run configurations of Problem~\ref{prob:sp}, beginning
with those in which consumption remains bounded away from zero,
$\liminf_{t\to\infty}C>0$. The organising object is
the fate of material input: within the regular class of paths fixed below, $R$ either settles at
a positive level, vanishes, or grows without bound, and where it settles, extraction $N$ either
dies or survives. That yields exactly four configurations --- the \emph{stationary state}, the
\emph{balanced circular path}, \emph{materially expanding growth}, and \emph{dematerialised
consumption} --- and the section is built around that fact. The technology
trends~\eqref{eq:sp:setup:tfp} run through the whole section: results are stated for general
$\left(g_A,g_B\right)\ge0$, organised so that the stationary-technology case $g_A=g_B=0$ ---
where the full proofs live --- is the backbone, and the trending economy is handled by a
growth-structure lemma, a cell-by-cell classification at the rate level, and a parametric
closure under the workhorse family.
Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:setup} defines the four paths and fixes the assumptions and growth
notation maintained throughout. Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:classification} states the growth
structure (Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:branches}), the classification theorem at stationary
technology, and its trending counterpart (Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:trend}): the four
configurations are the only sustained-consumption limits, and each one forces the rest of its
configuration's structure.
Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:selection} gives the selection map: at stationary technology three
primitives --- a growth threshold, the tail of the discovery cost, and the essentiality of
materials --- decide which configuration the economy is on; under trend growth the map is
rebuilt around the trend vector. Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:paths} then works through the four
paths in turn: two are fully characterised at stationary technology (the stationary state and
the balanced circular path), two are placed and constrained but left open in profile at this
level of generality (materially expanding growth and dematerialised consumption); the
workhorse family closes MEG's profile in parametric form (Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:wh:meg})
and carries the trending rate systems (Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:wh:trend}).
Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:collapse} then completes the map with the collapse paths,
$C\to0$: the fate of the cells that the four sustained configurations leave empty. Results
are stated with a sketch of the mechanism; all derivations and proofs are in
Appendices~\ref{sec:app:longrun} and~\ref{sec:app:collapse}, with the trending derivations
in~\ref{sec:app:longrun:trend} and~\ref{sec:app:workhorse:trend}.

\subsection{Configurations, maintained assumptions, and notation}
\label{sec:sp:longrun:setup}

\smalltitle{Technology trends and effective magnitudes.} The trends of~\eqref{eq:sp:setup:tfp}
are carried throughout: $A=e^{g_At}$ Hicks-neutral, $B=e^{g_Bt}$ material-augmenting,
$g_A,g_B\ge0$. Write
\begin{align}
m\equiv BR
\label{eq:sp:lr:services}
\end{align}
for \emph{material services}, the effective quantity the kernel sees; $R$ remains the physical
tonnage, and every mass balance, every configuration definition, and every material margin
below is a statement about tonnes. Kernel partials are written $F_1,F_2,F_3$ as
in~\eqref{eq:sp:setup:mpconvention}, so $F_K=AF_1$ and $F_R=ABF_2$. Two levels of rigour
coexist in this section and are flagged throughout: at stationary technology, $g_A=g_B=0$,
the results carry the full arguments of Appendices~\ref{sec:app:longrun}
and~\ref{sec:app:collapse}; under trend growth the results are established at the level of
rates and the count of level conditions (the standard this documentation applied to MEG
before the workhorse closed it), with derivations in~\ref{sec:app:longrun:trend}
and~\ref{sec:app:workhorse:trend} and the remaining holes collected as
gaps~(xvi)--(xix) in~\ref{sec:app:longrun:gaps}.

\smalltitle{Growth notation.} For any variable or price $Z$ of eventually constant sign, write
\begin{align}
g_Z\equiv\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\dot Z}{Z}
\label{eq:sp:lr:gZ}
\end{align}
for its asymptotic (exponential) growth rate, whenever the limit exists. Rates are signed ---
decline is $g_Z<0$ --- and the definition covers negative prices unchanged, since
$\dot Z/Z=d\ln\left|Z\right|/dt$ for $Z$ of constant sign. One convention matters throughout:
$g_Z=0$ does \emph{not} mean that $Z$ settles, only that it moves subexponentially, and a
variable can still diverge that way --- cumulative discovery grows linearly in the stationary
state, and recycling capital grows linearly on the balanced circular path under an exponential
yield tail, both with $g_Z=0$. Where the distinction matters, the text flags it.

\smalltitle{The four configurations.} Each definition is deliberately minimal: it records only
the fate of $\left(R,N\right)$. The familiar structure of each path --- exact exhaustion, deep
circularity, constancy of prices, the discovery regime --- is not written into the definitions
but \emph{forced} onto them by Theorem~\ref{thm:sp:lr:classification} below.

\begin{definition}[Stationary state]\label{def:sp:sss}
A sustained-consumption solution follows a \emph{stationary state} (SS) if $R$ is bounded away
from zero and infinity and $N\to\bar N>0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Balanced circular path]\label{def:sp:bcp}
A sustained-consumption solution follows a \emph{balanced circular path} (BCP) if $R$ is
bounded away from zero and infinity and $N\to0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Materially expanding growth]\label{def:sp:meg}
A sustained-consumption solution follows \emph{materially expanding growth} (MEG) if
$R\to\infty$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Dematerialised consumption]\label{def:sp:dc}
A sustained-consumption solution follows \emph{dematerialised consumption} (DC) if $R\to0$.
\end{definition}

In economic terms: on the SS, extraction and partial circularity continue forever and a
positive pollution stock is part of the long run; on the BCP the economy asymptotically
extracts nothing, treats everything, recycles everything, and pollution vanishes; on MEG the
material side expands with the economy and scarcity never binds --- the cornucopian
configuration; on DC the economy outgrows its material side entirely and lives on capital
alone.

\smalltitle{Materials in production.} The role of materials enters through two properties of
very different strength, plus a saturation condition and, for the trending analysis, a
regularity condition on the kernel's tails. All four are stated on the kernel $F$ --- the
time-invariant object --- so that the trends never contaminate the primitives.

\begin{assumption}[Materials in production]\label{ass:sp:lr:F}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)] \emph{Marginal indispensability:} $F_2\to\infty$ along any path with $K\to\infty$
    and $m$ bounded (in particular whenever $K/m\to\infty$).
    \item[(ii)] The asymptotic kernel return
    \begin{align}
    A_\infty\equiv\lim_{K\to\infty}F_1\left(K,m,0\right)
    \label{eq:sp:lr:Ainfty}
    \end{align}
    exists for each fixed $m>0$; the asymptotic return of the \emph{technology} is
    $A\left(t\right)A_\infty$, and the two coincide at stationary
    technology.\footnote{When the limit depends on $m$ or on the pollution
    stock, $A_\infty$ is understood at the limiting $\left(m,P\right)$ of the path in
    question; for CES kernels it is independent of $m$. The $P$-dependence matters only
    on MEG: the BCP and DC have $P\to0$, and the SS never evaluates $F_1$ at unbounded $K$.}
    \item[(iii)] \emph{Saturation:} $F_2\left(K,m,P\right)\to0$ as $m\to\infty$ for fixed $K$.
    \item[(iv)] \emph{Trend-regular kernel} (invoked only when
    $\left(g_A,g_B\right)\ne\left(0,0\right)$): the kernel is constant returns in
    $\left(K^Y,m\right)$, with intensive form $f\left(k\right)\equiv F\left(k,1,P\right)$ at
    the path's limiting pollution stock, $k\equiv K^Y/m$ the \emph{effective} capital
    intensity, and the tail objects
    \begin{align}
    \beta_\infty\equiv\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{f'\left(k\right)k}{f\left(k\right)}\in\left[0,1\right),
    \qquad
    \epsilon_\infty\equiv-\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{d\ln f'\left(k\right)}{d\ln k}\in\left(0,\infty\right],
    \qquad
    \gamma_A\equiv1+\frac{\beta_\infty}{\epsilon_\infty}\ge1,
    \label{eq:sp:lr:kernel-tails}
    \end{align}
    exist, with the consistency condition $\epsilon_\infty\ge1-\beta_\infty$ (which is what
    keeps investment from outrunning output on the trend-paced branch below;
    Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:trend}). We call $\gamma_A$ the \emph{trend multiplier}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[Asymptotic essentiality]\label{def:sp:lr:essential}
Materials are \emph{asymptotically essential} if no feasible path of Problem~\ref{prob:sp}
sustains $\liminf Y>0$ with $R\to0$.
\end{definition}

Five clarifications, because the distinctions carry the results. First, (i) holds for every CES
kernel with substitution elasticity at or above one --- \emph{including} above one, where
materials are inessential: unbounded capital drives output up, and the material margin is
pulled up with it --- and for Cobb--Douglas. It fails for (asymptotically) linear kernels
in which capital is a perfect substitute for materials, and it also fails \emph{below} unit
elasticity, where output is capped by the material leg and $F_2$ tends to the finite constant
$b_R^{\varsigma/\left(\varsigma-1\right)}$ (in the CES notation of
Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:workhorse}). The failure below one is harmless: there $A_\infty=0$
confines the economy to the bounded or trend-paced branch, and (i) is invoked only on the
endogenous growth branch.
Every result below on the resource side uses only (i). Second, asymptotic essentiality is
strictly stronger than the pointwise condition $F\left(K,0,P\right)=0$, and it depends on
depreciation: Cobb--Douglas satisfies it when $\delta>0$ (holding output up while $R\to0$
requires $K\to\infty$ with an unaffordable maintenance bill $\delta K$) but not when $\delta=0$
--- the classic constant-consumption results with vanishing resource use live at zero
depreciation. A CES technology is asymptotically essential below unit elasticity ($F$ is
eventually bounded by a multiple of $R$, and $A_\infty=0$) and not above it (where
$F\left(K,0,\cdot\right)$ is linear in $K$ and $A_\infty>0$). Third, the two properties do
different work in what follows: (i) drives all results on the resource side, while essentiality
is interrogated only when the material side winds down, where it separates collapse from
dematerialised consumption. The balanced circular path sits in between: it requires growth, and
under essentiality that combination ($A_\infty>0$ \emph{and} essential materials) is delivered
by no unmodified textbook form --- families such as $F=\Lambda\left(R\right)K+\text{lower-order terms}$ with
$\Lambda(0)=0$ do, materials capping the productivity of capital rather than entering as a
substitutable factor, and so does a CES above unit elasticity with a \emph{material floor},
$F$ depending on $R-\underline R$ and vanishing below it
(Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:workhorse}). Essentiality itself is \emph{not} assumed in what follows; where it
matters, the statements say so. Fourth, saturation (iii) also has a CES boundary: it holds
below unit elasticity but fails above it, where $F_2\to b_R^{\varsigma/\left(\varsigma-1\right)}$
as $m\to\infty$ --- in bulk, materials become a linear stand-alone technology. Where (iii) is
invoked (the bounded-branch exclusion of MEG), Appendix~\ref{sec:app:workhorse} supplies the
substitute condition: bulk materials must not out-earn their own collection cost,
$b_R^{\varsigma/\left(\varsigma-1\right)}<c^c$. Fifth, essentiality is trend-sensitive in a
way the other properties are not. Definition~\ref{def:sp:lr:essential} quantifies over the
feasible paths of the actual problem, trends included, so it generalises without rewording ---
but its verdict moves: with $g_B>0$ a kernel that is essential in the stationary sense
($\varsigma<1$, say) no longer makes materials essential \emph{as a path property}, because
material services $m=BR$ can grow while tonnage $R$ dies --- the classic resource-augmenting
escape of the exhaustible-resource literature (Dasgupta--Heal, Stiglitz), here in
material-services form. Distinguish therefore \emph{kernel essentiality} (the stationary
notion, read off $F$) from \emph{path essentiality} (the definition); they coincide at
$g_B=0$, and the only kernel feature that implies path essentiality at every trend vector is
the \emph{hard} floor, $F=0$ below $\underline R$ tonnes: a floor in tonnage cannot be
augmented away, which is precisely why the workhorse's floor is specified on $R$, not on $m$
(Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:workhorse}).

\smalltitle{Discovery-cost regimes.} The second primitive of the selection map below is the
tail of the discovery cost.

\begin{assumption}[Discovery-cost regimes]\label{ass:sp:lr:discovery}
Exactly one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(E)] \emph{Exhaustible discovery:} $C^D_D\left(0,X\right)\to\infty$ as
    $X\to\bar X_{\max}<\infty$;
    \item[(I)] \emph{Inexhaustible discovery:} $C^D\left(D,X\right)\to\bar C^D\left(D\right)$
    with $C^D_X\to0$ as $X\to\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
Costs rise with cumulative discovery in both regimes ($C^D_X>0$ throughout); the regimes differ
in whether the rise saturates at a finite replacement-cost schedule $\bar C^D\left(D\right)$ ---
a backstop, as when ever-lower grades are available in unbounded quantity --- or diverges at a
finite endowment $\bar X_{\max}$.
\end{assumption}

\smalltitle{Scoping.} Finally, the class of paths we classify, and three side conditions that
remove degenerate corners.

\begin{assumption}[Regular limits and maintained side conditions]\label{ass:sp:lr:regular}
We classify within the class of solutions in which every state, control and normalised price
converges in the extended sense, to a point of $\left[-\infty,\infty\right]$; this excludes
persistent cycles by assumption, not by proof. In addition we maintain:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)] \emph{Knife-edges aside:} the branch comparisons of
    Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:branches} are strict --- at stationary technology
    $A_\infty-\delta\ne\rho$, and in general
    $A_\infty-\delta\ne\rho+\eta\left(g_B+g_R\right)$ at the relevant configuration rates. See
    Remark~\ref{rem:sp:lr:exponential} for exactly what this excludes.
    \item[(ii)] \emph{Reserve depletion:} $C^N_N\left(0,S\right)\to\infty$ as $S\to0$, with the
    domain restriction $S\ge0$. Absent this, a zero-stock flow-through configuration --- $S=0$
    reached in finite time with extraction running on just-in-time discovery, $D=N>0$ ---
    reappears as a corner variant of the stationary state. In addition, the reserve is
    \emph{cumulatively} protected: $\int_0^\varepsilon C^N_N\left(0,s\right)ds=\infty$, so
    exhaustion cannot complete in finite time (workhorse: $\chi\ge1$; the matched BCP
    satisfies this automatically, since $\chi=\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi>1$). Absent the second
    clause, a finite-time-exhaustion variant --- extraction locked at its corner behind the
    depletion barrier, the loop financed by storage release --- evades the per-date
    interiority of Theorem~\ref{thm:sp:lr:classification}(ii); see
    gap~(xv) of Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:gaps}.
    \item[(iii)] \emph{Damage dominance:} along any path with $C\to\infty$ and $P\to\infty$,
    flow utility $u\left(C\right)-v\left(P\right)\to-\infty$. Automatic when $\eta>1$, since
    $u$ is then bounded above (at $\eta=1$, $u=\ln C$ is not, and the condition becomes a joint
    restriction on the speeds of $C$ and $P$).
    \item[(iv)] \emph{Viable material margin:} at any bounded candidate limit with $R\to0$, a
    virgin tonne available at bounded cost is worth its cost, so the material side reopens.
    Concretely: the extraction corner~\eqref{eq:sp:sum:N-corner} fails at the candidate limit
    whenever $S$ is bounded away from zero; and under regime (I) the corner
    conditions~\eqref{eq:sp:sum:N-corner} and~\eqref{eq:sp:sum:D-corner} cannot hold jointly
    at the limit, since a tonne can first be discovered at bounded cost and then extracted.
    This is a restriction on endogenous limits rather than on primitives; it scopes out the
    economies in which materials are never worth their supply cost even from the first tonne
    --- internally consistent, but with no material side to study.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\subsection{The classification theorem}\label{sec:sp:longrun:classification}

The workhorse lemma collects what every sustained-consumption limit must satisfy, with the
scope of each item stated explicitly --- the third item belongs to the bounded-material paths
only, and its failure mode on MEG is itself informative.

\begin{lemma}[Common necessary conditions]\label{lem:sp:lr:common}
Any solution with $\liminf C>0$ satisfies, as $t\to\infty$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{The growth dichotomy (stationary technology).} At $g_A=g_B=0$ the threshold
    decides the branch (Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:branches} is the general statement). If
    $A_\infty-\delta>\rho$, the capital stock is unbounded and the economy is asymptotically an
    $AK$ economy:
    \begin{align}
    r\to A_\infty-\delta,
    \qquad
    g_C=g_K=g_Y=\frac{A_\infty-\delta-\rho}{\eta}>0
    \label{eq:sp:lr:gC}
    \end{align}
    whenever $R$ is bounded; on MEG the same holds with $A_\infty$ replaced by the limit of
    $F_K$ along the path, no smaller than $A_\infty$ when $F_{KR}\ge0$. If $A_\infty-\delta<\rho$, the economy is bounded: $K$, $C$,
    $Y$ converge to positive constants and $r\to\rho$.
    \item \emph{A stable pollution sink.} $\Xi\to\bar\Xi<\infty$ with
    $\theta\left(\bar P\right)\bar P=\bar\Xi$, $\rho+\theta+\theta'\bar P>0$, and
    $\theta\left(P\right)P$ increasing at $\bar P$ (no terminal tipping).
    \item \emph{The bounded material side (SS and BCP only).} If $R$ is bounded away from zero
    and infinity: $W$ is bounded away from zero, net absorption of material into the capital
    stock dies, $\dot M^K\to0$, and the ledger~\eqref{eq:sp:setup:ledger-collected} locks the
    circularity gap to the virgin inflow:
    \begin{align}
    W\left(1-a\varpi\right)\;\sim\;\left(1+\Omega^{N,S}\right)N+\Omega^{D,S}D .
    \label{eq:sp:lr:ratelock}
    \end{align}
    \emph{Full circularity ($a\varpi\to1$) and vanishing extraction are the same event}, and
    they require both margins at their boundaries: $\varpi\to1$, $a\to1$, hence $x\to\infty$
    and $K^R\to\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Item (i) is the master split: every sustained-consumption limit lives on one side of a single
technological threshold, and the side is not chosen but determined. Item (iii) is the
structural heart of the two bounded-material paths: an economy that keeps its material side
alive cannot let throughput die, so whatever happens to extraction must be mirrored, tonne for
tonne, by the circularity gap. Note what item (iii) does \emph{not} cover: on MEG, $\dot M^K$
need not die --- storage in the capital stock is exactly how that path absorbs any unbounded
virgin inflow while keeping its sink stable (Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:meg}).

Under trend growth the dichotomy of item (i) is replaced by a four-way structure. The lemma
is stated at the rate level; Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:trend} carries the derivation and
the level conditions.

\begin{lemma}[Growth structure under technology trends]\label{lem:sp:lr:branches}
Consider a regular solution with $\liminf C>0$ whose physical material input has asymptotic
rate $g_R$, so material services grow at $g_B+g_R$. Assume the wedges $\zeta\Omega^j$
converge and $q\to1$ (part of regularity on these paths;
Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:trend}). Then exactly one of the following describes the
asymptotics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Explosive (excluded):} $g_A>0$ and $A_\infty>0$. Since $F_{11}<0$ gives
    $F_1>A_\infty$ at every finite $K$, the return $r\to\infty$ and the consumption
    rule~\eqref{eq:sp:sum:Cgrowth} forces $\dot C/C\to\infty$: growth is
    super-exponential, and \emph{no regular exponential path exists in the cell at all} ---
    sustained or collapsing. These economies leave the classified class the way cycles do,
    by scope; see gap~(xvii) for the caveats (damage-choked and wedge-choked variants).
    \item \emph{Endogenous growth:} $A_\infty>0$, $g_A=0$, and
    \begin{align}
    A_\infty-\delta>\rho+\eta\left(g_B+g_R\right).
    \label{eq:sp:lr:effthreshold}
    \end{align}
    The effective intensity $k=K^Y/m$ diverges, capital outruns material services, and the
    stationary-technology asymptotics of Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:common}(i) apply unchanged:
    $r\to A_\infty-\delta$, $g_C=g_K=g_Y=\left(A_\infty-\delta-\rho\right)/\eta>g_B+g_R$.
    The trend $g_B$ never reaches the growth rate; it enters only the material-side rates.
    \item \emph{Trend-paced growth:} $A_\infty=0$ (any $g_A,g_B$), or $A_\infty>0$ with
    $g_A=0$ and the inequality in~\eqref{eq:sp:lr:effthreshold} reversed. Under the
    trend-regular kernel (Assumption~\ref{ass:sp:lr:F}(iv)), the capital margin settles at
    $Af'\left(k\right)\to\rho+\delta+\eta g_C$ --- $k$ converging when $g_A=0$, deepening at
    $g_k=g_A/\epsilon_\infty$ when $g_A>0$ --- and consumption is paced by the technology:
    \begin{align}
    g_C=g_Y=\gamma_Ag_A+g_B+g_R,
    \qquad
    g_K=\frac{g_A}{\epsilon_\infty}+g_B+g_R\;\le\;g_Y,
    \qquad
    r\to\rho+\eta g_C,
    \label{eq:sp:lr:pace}
    \end{align}
    the inequality by the consistency condition $\epsilon_\infty\ge1-\beta_\infty$ (strict
    when $\beta_\infty=0$, where the capital-output ratio and the investment share vanish;
    equality in the Cobb--Douglas case, where they settle).
    \item \emph{Stationary:} $g_A=g_B=0$, $A_\infty-\delta<\rho$, $g_R=0$: the bounded branch
    of Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:common}(i), $r\to\rho$, all quantities settling.
\end{enumerate}
In compact form, away from the explosive cell and the knife-edges,
\begin{align}
g_C=\max\left\{\frac{A_\infty-\delta-\rho}{\eta},\;\gamma_Ag_A+g_B+g_R\right\},
\label{eq:sp:lr:gCmax}
\end{align}
with the first entry read as $-\infty$ when $A_\infty=0$: consumption grows at the faster of
the endogenous rate and the trend pace.
\end{lemma}

Two readings. First, \emph{the growth threshold becomes an effective
threshold}~\eqref{eq:sp:lr:effthreshold}: the trend $g_B+g_R$ raises the return the Euler
equation demands of a trend-paced path, so self-sustaining accumulation must now beat not
$\rho$ but $\rho$ plus the utility cost of the growth the trends already deliver. Second, on
the trend-paced branch \emph{the return is no longer a technological datum}:
$r=\rho+\eta g_C$ is set by preferences and the pace, and the technology adjusts through $k$
--- the mirror image of the endogenous branch, where the technology sets $r$ and consumption
adjusts.

A second general lemma carries the environmental side of the trending economy. At stationary
technology, only the growth cells had to keep their damages tame; under trends every
sustained cell grows, and the discipline becomes global.

\begin{lemma}[Affluence prices pollution]\label{lem:sp:lr:affluence}
On any regular sustained-consumption path with $g_C>0$, under $\eta>1$, $c^T\left(1\right)<\infty$
and $d^W>0$: no positive limiting pollution stock exists, $P\to0$. Mechanism: with
$P\to\bar P>0$, marginal damage in goods units,
$\mathcal V\ge v'\left(\bar P\right)/\lambda$, diverges at rate $\eta g_C$, and $p^P$ and the
emission component of $p^W$ with it, while every delivered-material value grows at most at
the material-price pace $\pi\le g_C$ (the growth rate of $F_R$ along the path, made explicit
in~\eqref{eq:sp:lr:pace-price} and Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:trend}); the treatment
margin~\eqref{eq:sp:sum:varpi} then reaches $\varpi=1$ and the extraction and exploration
margins~\eqref{eq:sp:sum:N-corner}--\eqref{eq:sp:sum:D-corner} their corners in finite time,
all inflows die, the loop runs down on a bounded storage release, cumulative emissions are
finite, and the sink drains $P$ to zero --- a contradiction. Consequently the damage-rate
conditions ($\beta_v$, $\beta_F$ analogues) that at stationary technology discipline only
the BCP and MEG become conditions on \emph{every} trending configuration, with the
configuration's own rates inserted (Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:wh:trend}).
\end{lemma}

The economics is a wealth effect with a sharp rate: marginal utility of consumption collapses
at rate $\eta g_C$ while material values track at most $g_C$, so the relative price of a clean
environment rises at the gap $\left(\eta-1\right)g_C>0$. A growing economy always ends clean;
a \emph{polluted} long run ($\bar P>0$) is possible only where nothing grows --- it is a
stationary-technology artefact, and the stationary state of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:sss} is exactly that case.

\begin{theorem}[Classification of sustained-consumption limits]
\label{thm:sp:lr:classification}
At stationary technology ($g_A=g_B=0$), under
Assumptions~\ref{ass:sp:lr:F}--\ref{ass:sp:lr:regular}, every solution of
Problem~\ref{prob:sp} with $\liminf_{t\to\infty}C>0$ follows exactly one of the four
configurations of Definitions~\ref{def:sp:sss}--\ref{def:sp:dc}, and each configuration carries
the following forced structure:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{SS} requires a bounded economy and inexhaustible discovery:
    $A_\infty-\delta<\rho$ and regime (I). All controls, states and prices other than $X$
    converge to constants, with
    \begin{align*}
    \bar D=\bar N>0,\qquad\bar S>0,\qquad\bar a\bar\varpi<1,\qquad\bar P>0,
    \end{align*}
    while $X\to\infty$ linearly at rate $\bar N$ ($g_X=0$).
    \item \emph{BCP} requires growth and exhaustible discovery: $A_\infty-\delta>\rho$ and
    regime (E). Extraction, discovery and the reserve vanish with $N,D,S>0$ at every date,
    exhaustion is exact,
    $\int_0^\infty N\,dt=S_0+\int_0^\infty D\,dt$ with $S\to0$ and $X\to\bar X_{\max}<\infty$,
    and circularity is deep: $\varpi\to1$, $a\to1$, $K^R\to\infty$, with the pollution stock
    vanishing.
    \item \emph{MEG} requires growth and inexhaustible discovery: $A_\infty-\delta>\rho$ and
    regime (I).
    \item \emph{DC} requires materials that are not asymptotically essential and exhaustible
    discovery: regime (E), with dematerialisation possible only \emph{after} asymptotic
    exhaustion --- $S\to0$ with exact exhaustion, reserves never stranded. Under asymptotic
    essentiality the configuration is empty.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proof (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:classification}) runs on three levers, worth seeing in
the open. First, the growth threshold determines the branch (Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:common}(i));
there is no choice involved. Second, regime (E) makes cumulative virgin input finite,
$\int_0^\infty N\,dt\le S_0+\bar X_{\max}$, so in the regular class extraction must die:
\emph{permanent extraction requires the backstop}. Third, regime (I) keeps the virgin tonne
available forever at bounded replacement cost, which cuts the other way: an economy facing the
backstop never abandons materials (a diverging $F_R$ against a bounded supply cost revives
extraction), and under growth it cannot hold the material side fixed either --- so growth with
the backstop is MEG, and DC can live only under regime (E). What remains in each cell is then
settled by the configurations the optimality conditions rule out along the way: reserves are
never stranded (deep circularity drives $p^W\to-\infty$ while a bounded reserve keeps
extraction costs bounded, so the virgin margin revives and optimality selects exact
exhaustion); extraction and exploration never hit terminal corners on circular paths (the
corner inequalities fail in finite time against diverging prices); and circularity without
growth is impossible (maintenance of the unbounded $K^R$ is unaffordable in a bounded economy,
and the diverging $\zeta$ breaks the consumption condition~\eqref{eq:sp:sum:C}) --- which is
why the BCP must grow and the SS must remain partially circular forever.

\begin{remark}[Exponential growth is derived, not assumed]\label{rem:sp:lr:exponential}
The exponential benchmark is not a restriction smuggled in through
Assumption~\ref{ass:sp:lr:regular}. On the unbounded branch, existence of $A_\infty$
(Assumption~\ref{ass:sp:lr:F}(ii)) delivers $r\to A_\infty-\delta$, and the consumption
rule~\eqref{eq:sp:sum:Cgrowth} then forces $\dot C/C\to\left(A_\infty-\delta-\rho\right)/\eta$
--- a \emph{positive constant} whenever the threshold is strict, so asymptotically exponential
growth is a conclusion. Sustained but subexponential growth ($C\to\infty$ with $\dot C/C\to0$)
requires $r\to\rho$, which is exactly the knife-edge $A_\infty-\delta=\rho$; the polynomial and
quasi-arithmetic growth paths of the classic exhaustible-resource literature all live there,
and only there. The same confinement holds on the material side: hyperbolic extraction profiles
are FOC-consistent only at the knife-edge (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:matching}). Setting
the knife-edge aside therefore \emph{is} the exponentiality assumption, and it excludes a
single point of the parameter space. Under trend growth, exponentiality acquires a second,
more prosaic source: the trends are exponential by specification, and the trend-paced
rates~\eqref{eq:sp:lr:pace} are inherited from them; the knife-edge generalises to the
effective threshold~\eqref{eq:sp:lr:effthreshold} holding with equality, and the explosive
cell of Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:branches}(i) is the new exit from the exponential class ---
from above rather than below. What genuinely remains open is subtler: slowly varying
corrections ($t^\beta e^{gt}$) to the exponential rates, and paths on which $F_1$ has no limit
at all (failure of Assumption~\ref{ass:sp:lr:F}(ii)); see
Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:gaps}.
\end{remark}

The trending counterpart of the classification theorem can now be assembled from the two
lemmas. It is stated at the rate level: exhaustiveness is inherited from regularity, the
per-cell structure from the price races, and what is \emph{not} yet established --- level
systems, existence beyond rate-and-count --- is flagged in gaps~(xvi)--(xix).

\begin{proposition}[Classification under trend growth]\label{prop:sp:lr:trend}
Let $\left(g_A,g_B\right)\ne\left(0,0\right)$, $\eta>1$, and exclude the explosive cell
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:branches}(i)). Within the regular class, every sustained-consumption
solution of Problem~\ref{prob:sp} follows one of the four configurations of
Definitions~\ref{def:sp:sss}--\ref{def:sp:dc}, with consumption growing at the
rate~\eqref{eq:sp:lr:gCmax} evaluated at the configuration's $g_R$, and:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{The stationary state is empty.} No trending path sustains $N\to\bar N>0$:
    under regime (E) cumulative virgin input is finite, and under regime (I) the delivered
    value of a tonne grows at the material-price pace $\pi>0$ against a virgin supply cost
    bounded by the backstop, so a stationary extraction margin cannot stay balanced --- the
    material side must expand. Trend growth does to the SS cell exactly what endogenous
    growth did to its own: \emph{growth with the backstop is MEG}.
    \item \emph{Regime (I): materially expanding growth.} $R\to\infty$, deep circularity in
    relative terms, storage absorption, $P\to0$; admissible only under congestion and
    damage-rate conditions, which the workhorse makes parametric
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:wh:trend}). When they fail the cell admits no regular
    exponential limit (the trending twin of gap~(xii)).
    \item \emph{Regime (E): the winding-down column.} $N\to0$ with exact exhaustion, and the
    cell partitions as before --- BCP if the loop is worth maintaining ($R$ settles, with
    the matching structure of Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:bcp} at the pace $\pi$; the
    exponent matching $\chi=\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi$ is \emph{invariant} to the trends),
    DC if it is not and the kernel permits ($R\to0$, sustained precisely when the trends
    cover the decline, $\gamma_Ag_A+g_B+g_R\ge0$ --- open in profile), and collapse only
    when the material decline outruns the trends. Under the hard floor DC is closed and the
    BCP is anchored at $\underline R$; moreover the floor cell is no longer uniformly
    doomed: a trending closed-loop path at the floor is a rate-consistent candidate even
    where the stationary matching fails, because trend growth pays the loop's diverging
    bills (gap~(xviii)).
    \item \emph{Every cell ends clean:} $P\to0$ throughout
    (Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:affluence}), subject to the configuration's damage-rate
    conditions.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The map in one sentence: \emph{trend growth erases the stationary state and hands its cell to
materially expanding growth, keeps the winding-down column's exits but moves their boundaries
from matching knife-edges toward rate races against the trends, and forces every surviving
configuration to end clean.} The next subsection records both maps side by side.

\subsection{The selection map}\label{sec:sp:longrun:selection}

Theorem~\ref{thm:sp:lr:classification} and Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:trend} already contain
the selection logic; collecting it gives two maps. At stationary technology three primitives
place the economy: the growth threshold $A_\infty-\delta\gtrless\rho$, the
discovery regime (E)/(I), and --- in the (E) column only --- asymptotic essentiality.

\begin{center}
{\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{l|cc}
$g_A=g_B=0$ & (E) exhaustible discovery & (I) inexhaustible discovery \\
\hline
$A_\infty-\delta>\rho$ & \textbf{BCP}$^{\dagger}$ or DC$^{\ddagger}$ & \textbf{MEG} \\
$A_\infty-\delta<\rho$ & collapse or \textbf{DC}$^{\ddagger}$ & \textbf{SS} \\
\end{tabular}}
\end{center}

\noindent{\footnotesize $^{\dagger}$Under the matching conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:bcp}; when the matching fails, the exits partition by essentiality
--- DC (inessential) or, with essential materials, no damped regular path at all
(Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:collapse}). $^{\ddagger}$DC requires materials that are not
asymptotically essential; with essential materials the cell's exit is collapse, i.e.\ no
sustained-consumption limit --- damped and characterised when essentiality is soft,
catastrophic when it is hard (Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:collapse}).\par}

\medskip
Why essentiality is interrogated only in the (E) column: under the backstop the virgin tonne
never becomes unaffordable, so the economy never faces the question of living without materials
--- bounded economies settle into the SS and growing ones expand materially, essential or not.
Under regime (E) the material side must wind down (cumulative virgin input is finite), and
essentiality then decides the fate of consumption when it does: collapse if materials are
essential --- unless the economy can afford the balanced circular path, which is what the
matching conditions decide --- and dematerialised consumption if they are not.
Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:collapse} characterises the collapse exits.

\smalltitle{The trending map.} Under trend growth the map compresses: the growth-threshold row
disappears as a \emph{configuration} primitive (it survives inside the cells, deciding only
whether the rates are endogenous or trend-paced, Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:branches}), the SS cell
is handed to MEG, and essentiality is read as \emph{path} essentiality --- the floor, or a
kernel-essential technology whose decline the trends fail to cover.

\begin{center}
{\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular}{l|cc}
$\left(g_A,g_B\right)\ne\left(0,0\right)$ & (E) exhaustible discovery & (I) inexhaustible discovery \\
\hline
$g_A>0$ and $A_\infty>0$ & \multicolumn{2}{c}{explosive --- no regular exponential path (Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:branches}(i))} \\
otherwise & \textbf{BCP}$^{\dagger}$ or \textbf{DC}$^{\ddagger}$ or collapse$^{\P}$ & \textbf{MEG}$^{\S}$ \\
\end{tabular}}
\end{center}

\noindent{\footnotesize $^{\dagger}$Under the matching structure of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:bcp} at the material-price pace $\pi$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:wh:trend}); the exponent matching
$\chi=\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi$ is trend-invariant. Under the floor the BCP is anchored at
$\underline R$ and remains a candidate even where the matching fails (gap~(xviii)).
$^{\ddagger}$Requires no floor and trends that cover the decline,
$\gamma_Ag_A+g_B+g_R\ge0$; available under kernel essentiality whenever $g_B>0$ makes the
decline affordable --- the Stiglitz escape. $^{\P}$Only when the material decline outruns
the trends, $\gamma_Ag_A+g_B+g_R<0$; damped iff
$\left(\eta-1\right)\left|g_C\right|<\rho$, catastrophic under the floor when no
floor-anchored BCP exists (Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:collapse}).
$^{\S}$Under the congestion and damage-rate conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:wh:trend}; when they fail the cell admits no regular exponential
limit (trending twin of gap~(xii)).\par}

\medskip
Note what moved and what did not. The (E)/(I) column logic is untouched --- it is mass
accounting and backstop logic, blind to trends. The essentiality primitive weakened from a
kernel property to a rate race, and the growth threshold demoted from selecting
configurations to selecting rate regimes within them. The new primitive is the trend vector
itself: $\gamma_Ag_A+g_B$ is the pace the economy can grow \emph{on a settled material
base}, and every boundary in the trending map is some margin's race against it.

\begin{remark}[Functional forms select the limit]\label{rem:sp:lr:knife}
The BCP conditions (iv)--(vi) of Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:bcp} tie the tails of
$a\left(\cdot\right)$, $C^N$, $C^D$ and $v$ to one another exactly; a calibration will not
satisfy them by accident. Choosing functional forms for the quantitative model therefore
\emph{is} choosing a cell of the selection map --- most naturally the stationary state, which
requires no matching --- and the choice should be made deliberately.
Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:workhorse} makes the dial explicit. Under trend growth the
no-matching option moves with the map: the SS is unavailable, and the cell reachable without
exact matching is trending MEG (regime (I)), whose admissibility is the open congestion
region $\mu>\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi$ plus damage-rate checks
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:wh:trend}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The four paths}\label{sec:sp:longrun:paths}

\subsubsection{The stationary state}\label{sec:sp:longrun:path:sss}

The bottom-right cell is the neoclassical case. All prices are bounded, so none of the
fragilities of the BCP arise: the $\Phi^I$ treatment, the damage function at zero, and the cost
tails are all innocuous here.

\begin{proposition}[The stationary state]\label{prop:sp:lr:sss}
Under $A_\infty-\delta<\rho$, regime (I), and a stable sink
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:common}(ii)), a stationary state satisfies $r=\rho$ and:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Modified golden rule:} $F_Kw_Y=\rho+\delta-\Phi^I\left(1-\sigma\right)\rho\,p^W$,
    where $w_Y\equiv1-\zeta\Omega^Y$: the storage value of embodied material tilts the standard
    rule, towards more capital when waste is costly ($p^W>0$) and less when waste is an asset
    ($p^W<0$).
    \item \emph{Reserve-inventory condition:} combining the stationary rent with the exploration
    margin,
    \begin{align}
    \frac{\left|C^N_S\left(\bar N,\bar S\right)\right|w_N}{\rho}
    \;=\;\bar C^D_D\left(\bar N\right)w_D+p^W\Omega^{D,S}:
    \label{eq:sp:lr:inventory}
    \end{align}
    reserves are a produced inventory, held to the point where the annuitised extraction-cost
    saving of a marginal tonne in the ground equals its replacement cost through discovery.
    Hotelling's rent is pinned by replacement cost, not by dynamics.
    \item \emph{Treatment is always positive:} $\bar\varpi>0$, for the pollution motive alone if
    necessary.
    \item \emph{Recycling is active}, $\bar K^R>0$, if and only if $a'(0)V>F_Kw_Y$ at the
    candidate steady state; in particular always if $a'(0)=\infty$. When inactive, the treatment
    margin reduces to $p^P\left(1-d^W\right)=c^{T\prime}\left(\bar\varpi\right)w_W$.
\end{enumerate}
The stationary system closes (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:sss}); existence beyond the equation
count requires the stationary economy to afford its extraction, exploration and treatment bills
with $\bar C>0$, and uniqueness is not established.
\end{proposition}

\smalltitle{The SS under trends: dissolution.} The stationary state is the one configuration
with no trending counterpart (Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:trend}(i)): any trend makes the
delivered value of a tonne grow against a supply cost the backstop keeps bounded, and the
constant extraction margin that defines the SS cannot survive the imbalance --- the cell's
occupant becomes MEG. The point is worth stating in welfare terms: the SS is also the one
configuration whose long run is \emph{polluted}, $\bar P>0$, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:affluence} that outcome is possible only because nothing grows. A
polluted steady state is not a robust prediction of the model; it is the signature of an
economy whose technology has stopped.

\subsubsection{The balanced circular path}\label{sec:sp:longrun:path:bcp}

Beyond its cell --- growth and regime (E), already forced by
Theorem~\ref{thm:sp:lr:classification} --- the BCP requires a set of structural and tail
conditions; the tails are parametrised by
\begin{align}
1-a(x)\sim\gamma_ax^{-\psi},\quad\psi\in\left(0,\infty\right],
\qquad
C^N_N\left(0,S\right)\sim\kappa S^{-\chi},\quad\chi\ge0,
\label{eq:sp:lr:tails}
\end{align}
with $\psi=\infty$ denoting the exponential tail $1-a\sim e^{-x}$, and by the asymptotic growth
elasticity $e_R$ of $F_R$ along the growth path, $F_R\sim e^{e_Rg_Ct}$ (CES: $e_R=1/\varsigma$
for substitution elasticity $\varsigma$).

\begin{proposition}[The balanced circular path]\label{prop:sp:lr:bcp}
A solution converging to the BCP with asymptotically exponential rates exists only if all of the
following hold, and they jointly determine the path:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Dematerialising capital:} $\Phi^I=\Omega\phi^I$ with $\delta>0$. Under exogenous
    or constant $\Phi^I$ (Appendices~\ref{sec:app:exogenousPhiI}--\ref{sec:app:constantPhiI}),
    and likewise if $\delta=0$, the BCP is infeasible.
    \item \emph{Affordable full treatment:} $c^T(1)<\infty$.
    \item \emph{Pinned rates:} extraction and the reserve decline together at
    \begin{align}
    g_N=g_S=-\frac{\psi}{\psi+1}\,e_Rg_C,
    \label{eq:sp:lr:gN}
    \end{align}
    and all material prices --- $-p^W$, $-\zeta$, $p^S$, $C^N_N\left(0,S_t\right)$ --- diverge
    at the common rate
    \begin{align}
    g_{p^S}=e_Rg_C,
    \qquad\text{with}\qquad
    r\to A_\infty-\delta>g_{p^S}.
    \label{eq:sp:lr:gp}
    \end{align}
    \item \emph{Extraction-cost matching:} $\chi=\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi$.
    \item \emph{Discovery-cost matching:} the blow-up of $C^D_D\left(0,X\right)$ as
    $X\to\bar X_{\max}$ rate-matched to $p^S$.
    \item \emph{Tame damages:} $v'(0)=0$, with
    $\eta g_C-\min\left(\left|g_N\right|,\theta(0)\right)\le g_{p^S}$ and $\left|F_P\right|$
    growing slower than $e^{g_{p^S}t}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

One requirement is implicit in (iii) and worth surfacing: $e_R\le1$, so that
$g_{p^S}\le g_C$ and the material wedges $\zeta\Omega^j$ stay bounded (vanishing when strict);
CES satisfies it whenever the growth condition does, since $A_\infty>0$ requires
$\varsigma>1$ and hence $e_R=1/\varsigma<1$. The economics compresses into one sentence: on the
BCP \emph{the waste price carries the system} --- $p^W\to-\infty$, with $-p^W$ equal to the
marginal circularity multiplier $\left(1-\alpha\right)^{-1}$ times the per-pass surplus of a
tonne of waste, so waste becomes the economy's material asset (the mine above ground) --- and
every other condition in the list makes some margin's diverging cost keep pace with it.
Conditions (iv)--(vi) are knife-edges across independently specified primitives; see
Remark~\ref{rem:sp:lr:knife}.

\smalltitle{The BCP under trends.} All of the structure of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:bcp} survives trend growth with one substitution: the rate that
drives the price block is no longer $e_Rg_C$ but the \emph{material-price pace}
\begin{align}
\pi\;\equiv\;g_{F_R}\Big|_{\text{along the path}}
=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{g_C}{\varsigma}+\left(1-\dfrac{1}{\varsigma}\right)g_B
& \text{endogenous branch (CES, $R$ settled),}\\[0.8em]
g_C=\gamma_Ag_A+g_B
& \text{trend-paced branch ($R$ settled),}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:sp:lr:pace-price}
\end{align}
so that $g_N=g_S=-\frac{\psi}{\psi+1}\pi$, the material prices $-p^W$, $-\zeta$, $p^S$,
$C^N_N\left(0,S_t\right)$ diverge at $\pi$, and $K^R$ grows at $\pi/\left(\psi+1\right)$
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app:workhorse:trend}). Three consequences. First, the exponent matching
$\chi=\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi$ is \emph{invariant}: it relates the decay of $S$ to the
divergence of $C^N_N$, and both are paced by the same $\pi$, which cancels --- the BCP's
central knife-edge is a statement about tails, not about growth rates, and the trends cannot
soften it. Second, the trend-paced case opens the BCP to kernels the stationary theory
excluded: below unit elasticity, $A_\infty=0$, the stationary economy could not grow and
hence could not afford the loop; under trends it grows at
$\gamma_Ag_A+g_B$ and the loop's diverging bills are paid by the trend --- deep circularity
no longer requires capital to be self-sufficient. Third, the damage condition tightens: the
analogue of (vi) reads $\eta g_C-\min\left(\left|g_N\right|,\theta_0\right)\le\pi$, and
under trends $g_C$ rises with $\gamma_Ag_A+g_B$ while $\pi\le g_C$ --- faster growth makes
the clean limit harder to price, not easier, so a trending BCP demands strong damage tails
exactly where a stationary one demanded matched cost tails.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:sp:lr:neoclassical}
At stationary technology, under standard neoclassical assumptions ---
$\lim_{K\to\infty}F_1=0$, or $A_\infty-\delta<\rho$,
or a fixed material intensity of investment --- the balanced circular path does not exist. A
fully circular economy is not a stationary destination: it must be perpetually outgrown, and is
affordable only where capital accumulation is self-sustaining and capital itself
dematerialises. Trend growth removes the first two disqualifiers --- any positive trend
supplies the perpetual outgrowing --- but not the third: the dematerialising-capital
requirement ($\Phi^I=\Omega\phi^I$ with $\delta>0$) survives at every trend vector, because
it is a mass-balance requirement, not a growth requirement.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Materially expanding growth}\label{sec:sp:longrun:path:meg}

The top-right cell is the cornucopian configuration: capital accumulation is self-sustaining
and the crustal backstop keeps the replacement cost of the virgin tonne bounded forever, so the
diverging value of materials along the growth path is met not by diverging costs, as on the
BCP, but by expansion --- the economy mines and grows without end. The configuration is not
characterised in this paper, but it is constrained: its pollution problem admits only one
solution.

\begin{proposition}[Necessary conditions on MEG]\label{prop:sp:lr:meg}
On any MEG path (with $d^W>0$ and $\delta>0$):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Deep circularity in the limit:} $\varpi\to1$ and $a\to1$, with
    $\left(1-\varpi\right)W$ and $\left(1-a\right)W$ bounded, hence
    $W\left(1-a\varpi\right)$ bounded while $W\to\infty$.
    \item \emph{Storage absorption:} the ledger then forces
    $\dot M^K=\left(1+\Omega^{N,S}\right)N+\Omega^{D,S}D-W\left(1-a\varpi\right)$ with the last
    term bounded: any unbounded virgin inflow is absorbed, tonne for tonne, into the growing
    capital stock.
    \item \emph{Growth:} $K\ge K^R\to\infty$, and (for $F_{KR}\ge0$)
    $g_C\ge\left(A_\infty-\delta-\rho\right)/\eta>0$.\footnote{The bound reads the Euler
    equation~\eqref{eq:sp:sum:Cgrowth} with the wedge term gone: it presumes $\zeta\Omega^C$
    converges and $w_Y\to1$ (or $\zeta\le0$ in the limit, which gives $w_Y\ge1$ and preserves
    the inequality), and it reads $A_\infty$ at the path's limiting pollution stock. Both are
    parts of MEG's open price system (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:gaps}).}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The economics of (i)--(ii): with growing throughput, a stable pollution sink
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:common}(ii)) requires both the untreated share and the recycling losses
to vanish in absolute, not just relative, terms --- and what cannot be recycled must be
\emph{built}. The capital stock is the landfill: extraction on MEG is not a flow of materials
through the economy but a flow of materials into the economy's permanent structures, the mine
moving into the building stock. At this level of generality that is where matters rest: the
joint rates of $\left(N,D,S,W,K^R\right)$, the supporting price system, and whether the
optimality conditions admit the configuration at all are open
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:gaps}). Under the workhorse family all three close --- one
common material rate, a waste price that diverges here too, and a congestion threshold that
decides admissibility --- in Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:wh:meg} below.

Under trend growth MEG is promoted from one cell to the whole (I) column
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:trend}): every kernel expands materially against the backstop,
including those the stationary theory confined to the SS. The trending rate systems --- one
material rate on each branch, a looser sink threshold, the congestion brake balancing the
trend rather than the credit --- are parametric and collected in
Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:wh:trend}.

\subsubsection{Dematerialised consumption}\label{sec:sp:longrun:path:dc}

The DC path is the exit that opens when materials are not asymptotically essential: consumption
sustained on capital alone while material input dies. Dropping essentiality does not weaken any
of the resource results --- what it changes is only the fate of consumption after the material
side winds down.

\begin{proposition}[Placement of DC]\label{prop:sp:lr:dc}
Any DC path satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Only without essentiality:} under asymptotic essentiality no DC path exists.
    \item \emph{Only after exhaustion:} dematerialisation before exhaustion is ruled out by the
    same self-reviving logic as stranding --- while $S$ is bounded away from zero the value of
    the marginal tonne beats its bounded extraction cost (divergently so on the growth branch,
    where $F_R\to\infty$; by the viability condition on the bounded branch), the corner
    condition~\eqref{eq:sp:sum:N-corner} fails, and extraction revives to feed $R$. Hence
    exhaustion is exact, reserves are never stranded, and the discovery margin dies against the
    diverging cost exactly as on the BCP, $X\to\bar X_{\max}$.
    \item \emph{Only under regime (E):} under the backstop the revival argument applies at every
    date --- reserves are replenishable at bounded cost forever --- so materials are never
    abandoned.
\end{enumerate}
Two variants exist, split by the growth threshold: bounded DC ($A_\infty-\delta<\rho$), with
$C\to\bar C=F\left(\bar K,0,\bar P\right)-\delta\bar K>0$ and $r\to\rho$; and growing DC
($A_\infty-\delta>\rho$), asymptotically an $AK$ economy on capital alone with
$g_C=\left(A_\infty-\delta-\rho\right)/\eta$.
\end{proposition}

When does the economy take this exit rather than the BCP, when both are on the table? When the
matching conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:bcp} hold, the BCP is the FOC-consistent
limit and discarding materials while $F_R$ exceeds their recycled supply cost is not; when they
fail, maintaining the loop is not FOC-consistent, and the exits partition by essentiality:
collapse (essential) or DC (inessential, e.g.\ CES above unit elasticity). The profile of the
phase-out path --- the decay rates of $R$, $W$, $K^R$ and the associated price asymptotics ---
is left open (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:gaps}).

\smalltitle{DC under trends: the Stiglitz escape.} Trend growth transforms this
configuration more than any other except the SS. Consumption on a dematerialising path is
paced by~\eqref{eq:sp:lr:gCmax}: on the trend-paced branch
$g_C=\gamma_Ag_A+g_B+g_R$ with $g_R<0$, so the decline in tonnage is a direct deduction from
growth, and DC is a sustained-consumption configuration precisely when the trends cover the
decline, $\gamma_Ag_A+g_B+g_R\ge0$. Two consequences. First, DC opens to kernel-essential
technologies: with $g_B>0$, material services $m=BR$ can hold up or grow while tonnes die
--- the classic resource-augmenting escape, unavailable at any stationary technology, which
is why Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:dc}(i) reads ``essentiality'' as \emph{path}
essentiality: under trends only the floor closes the exit. Second, the boundary between DC
and collapse becomes a rate race rather than a property of the kernel: the same phase-out
profile is sustained consumption under strong trends and collapse under weak ones, with the
damped/catastrophic split of Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:collapse} applying on the collapse
side. What pins $g_R$ --- the phase-out profile itself --- remains the configuration's open
question, now under trends too (gap~(xix)).

\begin{remark}[The overburden channel]\label{rem:sp:lr:overburden}
The displaced-mass flows $\Omega^{N,S}N$ and $\Omega^{D,S}D$ enter the ledger as inflows that
never draw on $R$ (the ``manna'' caveat of Section~\ref{sec:sp:setup:materialaccounting}). Once
waste is an asset ($p^W<0$), these flows are \emph{credits} on the extraction and exploration
margins: in deep circularity the economy would value exploration partly as direct mass
harvesting. On the BCP this channel is priced and disciplined by the diverging discovery cost
(Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:bcp}(v)); in variants with bounded discovery costs it can become
the dominant motive, which signals a modelling artefact rather than economics. The stock-flow
caveat flagged in the setup is therefore substantive in the deep-circularity limit.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A workhorse family}\label{sec:sp:longrun:workhorse}

Remark~\ref{rem:sp:lr:knife} said that choosing functional forms is choosing a cell of the
selection map; this subsection makes the dial explicit. One parametric family covers every
primitive, built so that each object the theory runs on --- the growth threshold, the
discovery regime, essentiality, and the tail exponents $\psi$, $\chi$, $\mu$, $e_R$ --- is a
named parameter. Appendix~\ref{sec:app:workhorse} verifies the maintained assumptions region by
region (two require care), derives the tail mapping, works out what the material floor
$\underline R$ changes, and closes the MEG rate system;
Appendix~\ref{sec:app:workhorse:trend} derives the trending rate systems. The family is
written to double, later, as the specification of the quantitative model. The trend rates
$g_A,g_B\ge0$ of~\eqref{eq:sp:setup:tfp} are themselves parameters of the family: output is
$A\,F$ with the kernel below evaluated at material services $B\tilde R$, and --- deliberately
--- the floor is in \emph{tonnes}: $F=0$ whenever $R<\underline R$, so hard essentiality
cannot be augmented away (the fifth clarification of Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:setup}).

\smalltitle{What the floor buys.} The Stone--Geary term $\underline R>0$ speaks to the
limited-substitutability literature --- a minimum material throughput below which production
is impossible --- and it earns its place four times over
(derivations in Appendix~\ref{sec:app:workhorse:floor}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Essentiality with growth capacity, from a standard form.} With $\varsigma>1$
    and $\underline R>0$, $F_R\to\infty$ as $R\downarrow\underline R$ and output dies below the
    floor, so materials are asymptotically essential --- while
    $A_\infty=b_K^{\varsigma/\left(\varsigma-1\right)}>0$ is untouched. This is precisely the
    pairing that the clarifications of Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:setup} said requires
    nonstandard families. The floor acts as an Inada barrier: it never binds along an optimal
    path with active production.
    \item \emph{The DC exit closes.} $R\to0$ crosses the floor, so dematerialised consumption
    is unavailable and $\underline R$ becomes a fourth primitive of the selection map: with a
    floor, the (E) column exits are BCP or collapse only.
    \item \emph{The BCP's throughput is anchored by technology.} Diverging material prices
    push $R$ down to the floor, so $\bar R=\underline R$ and, with $N\to0$ and
    $a\varpi\to1$, $\bar W=\underline R$: the size of the eternal loop is a technological
    datum, replacing the delicate level-matching argument of
    Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:matching}.
    \item \emph{One knife-edge softens --- but only one.} The rate-pinning of
    Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:bcp}(iii) relaxes to an interval,
    $g_{p^S}\in\left[g_C/\varsigma,\,g_C\right]$, with the gap $\tilde R\to0$ absorbing the
    difference between the growth of $F_R$ and the growth of prices. The extraction matching
    $\chi=\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi$, however, \emph{persists}: it relates the scarcity block to
    the waste price and never involves $F_R$. Which point of the interval the optimum selects
    is open (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:longrun:gaps}).
\end{enumerate}
The floor also exacts a price. In every cell whose exit is collapse, hard essentiality turns
the collapse catastrophic: the loop cannot hold $R\ge\underline R$ on dying inflows,
production halts at a finite date, and lifetime utility is $-\infty$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:app:col:shutdown}). The damped collapse profiles of
Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:collapse} therefore require $\underline R=0$. Trend growth
mitigates the price without waiving it: a trending economy can hold the floor with a closed
loop whose bills the trend pays --- the floor-anchored BCP candidate of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:trend}(iii) --- so the catastrophic verdict applies only where
that candidate's conditions fail too (gap~(xviii)).

\smalltitle{Materially expanding growth: the congestion dial.} The workhorse also closes the
one configuration the general theory leaves open. On MEG the reserve never dies and the
scarcity tail $\chi$ goes quiet; what disciplines the virgin margin instead is the congestion
exponent $\mu$, and the whole configuration turns on it. Derivations are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:app:workhorse:meg}; the analysis maintains the baseline $\phi^I>0$ and sets
$\Omega^{D,S}=0$, since with a bounded discovery cost and a diverging waste asset any positive
exploration overburden makes exploration a direct mass-harvesting technology --- the artefact
of Remark~\ref{rem:sp:lr:overburden}, binding on this path.

\begin{proposition}[MEG under the workhorse]\label{prop:sp:wh:meg}
In the MEG cell ($\varsigma>1$, $b_K^{\varsigma/\left(\varsigma-1\right)}-\delta>\rho$,
discovery $\kappa_{\mathrm I}$), with $c_\mu>0$ and $\mu>0$, a MEG solution with
asymptotically exponential rates exists only if
\begin{align}
\mu>\frac{\psi+1}{\psi},
\qquad
\eta g_C-\beta_vm\;\le\;\mu g_m,
\qquad
g_C-\beta_Fm\;\le\;\mu g_m,
\label{eq:sp:wh:meg-conditions}
\end{align}
with $g_m$ and $m$ as below ($\mu>1$ under the exponential tail), and any such path
satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Exact growth at the undamaged return:} $P\to0$, all wedges vanish, and
    $g_C=\left(A_\infty-\delta-\rho\right)/\eta$ with
    $A_\infty=b_K^{\varsigma/\left(\varsigma-1\right)}$: the hedges of
    Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:meg}(iii) resolve and its growth bound holds with equality.
    \item \emph{One material rate:} $g_N=g_D=g_R=g_W=g_{M^K}=g_X=g_m$ with
    \begin{align}
    g_m=\frac{g_C}{1+\mu\varsigma}\;\in\;\left(0,g_C\right),
    \label{eq:sp:wh:gm}
    \end{align}
    absolute expansion with relative decoupling ($\Omega\to0$ at rate $g_C-g_m$), and a
    pinned virgin share,
    $N/R\to\bar\sigma g_m/\left[\left(g_m+\delta\right)\left(1+\Omega^{N,S}\right)\right]
    \in\left(0,1\right)$.
    \item \emph{Scarcity is inventory:} $p^S\to\bar\kappa$ and $p^X\to0$ --- the rent pinned
    by replacement cost, as in the stationary state --- while reserves grow as a produced
    inventory, $S\sim\left(\chi c_NN/\left(r\bar\kappa\right)\right)^{1/\left(1+\chi\right)}$,
    so $g_S=g_m/\left(1+\chi\right)$ and the reserve-to-extraction ratio falls.
    \item \emph{The waste asset carries MEG too:} $\varpi=1$ from a finite date, $-p^W$ and
    $-\zeta$ diverge at rate $\mu g_m$, and extraction is priced congestion against credit,
    $c_\mu N^\mu\sim\left(1+\Omega^{N,S}\right)\left(-p^W\right)$; the delivered-tonne value
    $\Psi$ diverges only at rate $\mu g_m/\left(\psi+1\right)$ (subexponentially under the
    exponential tail), and $K^R$ grows at $g_m\left(\psi+1+\mu\right)/\left(\psi+1\right)$,
    strictly between $g_m$ and $g_C$: the loop deepens against its own throughput while
    vanishing relative to the economy.
    \item \emph{Pollution dies:} $\Xi\sim e^{-g_\Xi t}$ with
    $g_\Xi=\frac{\mu\psi-\left(\psi+1\right)}{\psi+1}\,g_m>0$, and $P\to0$ at rate
    $m=\min\left(g_\Xi,\theta_0\right)$.
\end{enumerate}
When~\eqref{eq:sp:wh:meg-conditions} fails, the cell admits no regular exponential
sustained-consumption limit at all; what the optimum then does is open (gap~(xii)).
\end{proposition}

Three readings. First, the cornucopian path is not business as usual: it exists only where
extraction is congested steeply enough, and on it pollution vanishes, recycling is complete,
and the economy dematerialises in relative terms even as throughput explodes --- with every
net tonne extracted going into the built environment, now priced: the dominant motive for the
marginal tonne is its value as feedstock and structure, not its marginal product, which is why
extraction survives congestion at all. Second, waste is the economy's material asset on
\emph{both} growth configurations: $p^W\to-\infty$ on the BCP because throughput is scarce,
and on MEG because the virgin alternative is congested --- the mine above ground is not a
special feature of the circular path. Third, the threshold
$\mu>\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi$ is the BCP matching condition $\chi=\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi$
turned from a knife-edge into an open region: in both, the tail of the virgin brake is set
against the tail of the recycling yield, but depletion must match it exactly where congestion
need only beat it. The quantitative model inherits a clean menu: under regime (I) the economy
ends in the stationary state or, past the growth threshold, on a MEG path whose admissibility
is one parameter inequality.

\smalltitle{Two warnings from the verification.} First, the CES kernel fails one maintained
assumption on each side of unit elasticity: marginal indispensability
(Assumption~\ref{ass:sp:lr:F}(i)) below one --- harmless, as that region lives on the bounded
branch --- and saturation (Assumption~\ref{ass:sp:lr:F}(iii)) above one, where the
classification instead needs the bulk-materials condition
$b_R^{\varsigma/\left(\varsigma-1\right)}<c^c$: a tonne of materials, used essentially alone,
must not out-earn its own collection cost. Second, the common multiplicative-exponential
damage specification ($\beta_F=0$, i.e.\ $\Gamma'\!\left(0\right)>0$) kills both growth
configurations: marginal output damage is then proportional to output itself, the pollution
price outruns every material price, and extraction is forced to its corner --- on the BCP
directly, and on MEG through the damage conditions
of~\eqref{eq:sp:wh:meg-conditions}. For either circular growth path to exist, \emph{output}
damages, like utility damages, must have vanishing marginal at zero pollution. Under trend
growth this second warning sharpens from a growth-cell condition into a global one: every
trending cell grows (Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:branches}), so at $\beta_F=0$ \emph{no} regular
sustained configuration exists at any trend vector --- multiplicative-exponential damages and
technological progress are jointly incompatible with a regular long run in this family.

\smalltitle{The trending rate systems.} The trending map of
Section~\ref{sec:sp:longrun:selection} becomes parametric in the workhorse. Derivations are
in Appendix~\ref{sec:app:workhorse:trend}; existence is by rate-and-count throughout, the
same standard as Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:wh:meg}.

\begin{proposition}[Trending rate systems under the workhorse]\label{prop:sp:wh:trend}
Let $\left(g_A,g_B\right)\ne\left(0,0\right)$, $\eta>1$. In the workhorse family:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Branch placement.} $\varsigma>1$ with $g_A>0$ is explosive (no regular
    exponential path). $\varsigma>1$ with $g_A=0$ is endogenous iff
    $b_K^{\varsigma/\left(\varsigma-1\right)}-\delta>\rho+\eta\left(g_B+g_R\right)$ at the
    configuration's $g_R$, with $g_C=\left(A_\infty-\delta-\rho\right)/\eta$; otherwise, and
    for all $\varsigma\le1$, the economy is trend-paced with
    $g_C=\gamma_Ag_A+g_B+g_R$, where $\gamma_A=1$ for $\varsigma<1$ and $\gamma_A=1/\beta$
    for Cobb--Douglas with material share $\beta$.
    \item \emph{The BCP (regime (E), loop maintained).} The rate system of
    Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:bcp} holds with $e_Rg_C$ replaced by the pace $\pi$
    of~\eqref{eq:sp:lr:pace-price}: $g_N=g_S=-\frac{\psi}{\psi+1}\pi$, the material price
    block diverging at $\pi$, $g_{K^R}=\pi/\left(\psi+1\right)$. The matchings are
    trend-invariant --- $\chi=\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi$ and the discovery matching
    unchanged --- and the damage condition reads
    $\eta g_C-\min\left(\left|g_N\right|,\theta_0\right)\le\pi$ with $\beta_v,\beta_F>0$.
    On the trend-paced branch ($\varsigma\le1$: new territory) $\pi=g_C$; with the floor,
    $\bar R=\underline R$ anchors the loop and the interval logic of the floor subsection
    applies at the pace $\pi$.
    \item \emph{MEG (regime (I): the whole column).} Requires $c_\mu>0$, $\mu>0$, and the
    \emph{trend-invariant} congestion threshold $\mu>\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi$. One common
    material rate $g_N=g_D=g_R=g_W=g_{M^K}=g_m$:
    \begin{align}
    g_m=\frac{g_C+\left(\varsigma-1\right)g_B}{1+\mu\varsigma}
    \ \ \text{(endogenous branch)},
    \qquad
    g_m=\frac{\gamma_Ag_A+g_B}{\mu},
    \quad
    g_C=\left(1+\tfrac{1}{\mu}\right)\left(\gamma_Ag_A+g_B\right)
    \ \ \text{(trend-paced)},
    \label{eq:sp:wh:trend-gm}
    \end{align}
    with $-p^W$ and $-\zeta$ diverging at $\mu g_m$, $g_{K^R}=g_m\left(\mu+\psi+1\right)/\left(\psi+1\right)$,
    emissions dying at $g_\Xi=\left[\mu\psi-\left(\psi+1\right)\right]g_m/\left(\psi+1\right)$,
    $P\to0$ at $m_P=\min\left(g_\Xi,\theta_0\right)$, and the damage conditions
    $\eta g_C-\beta_vm_P\le\mu g_m$ and $g_C-\beta_Fm_P\le\mu g_m$. On the trend-paced
    branch this is the fate of the former SS cell: material throughput grows at
    $\left(\gamma_Ag_A+g_B\right)/\mu$ --- absolute decoupling exactly in the limit
    $\mu\to\infty$, relative decoupling (rate gap $g_C-g_m>0$) for every finite $\mu$.
    \item \emph{DC (regime (E), loop abandoned, $\underline R=0$).} Sustained iff the trends
    cover the decline: $g_C=\gamma_Ag_A+g_B+g_R\ge0$ on the trend-paced branch --- for
    $\varsigma\le1$ this is the Stiglitz escape, open only through $g_A,g_B>0$ --- and
    $g_C=\left(A_\infty-\delta-\rho\right)/\eta$ on the endogenous branch
    ($\varsigma>1$). The profile ($g_R$ itself) remains open (gap~(xix)).
\end{enumerate}
When the cell's conditions fail --- the congestion threshold, the matchings, or the damage
rates --- the cell admits no regular exponential sustained-consumption limit, and its fate is
open: the trending twins of gaps~(xii) and~(xiv).
\end{proposition}

Two features deserve emphasis, because they are the rebuild's substantive findings. First,
\emph{the structural knife-edges are trend-invariant}: the BCP's extraction matching
$\chi=\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi$ and MEG's congestion threshold $\mu>\left(\psi+1\right)/\psi$
survive every trend vector unchanged, because both are races between the loop's yield tail
and the virgin margin's tail, run at a common pace that cancels. Technology trends change how
fast the system moves, never which tails must beat which. Second, \emph{the damage-rate
conditions are not trend-invariant}: they tighten with $g_C$ on their left side while the
absorbing rates scale with the material block, so faster trends demand steeper damage tails
($\beta_v$, $\beta_F$) --- growth makes the clean limit obligatory
(Lemma~\ref{lem:sp:lr:affluence}) and simultaneously harder to support. The quantitative
model inherits the sharpened menu: under regime (I) with any technological progress, the
long run is trending MEG or nothing regular, and its admissibility is still one parameter
inequality plus damage-rate checks.

\subsection{Collapse}\label{sec:sp:longrun:collapse}

The four configurations exhaust the sustained-consumption limits, but not the selection map:
some cells offer no sustained exit at all. Within the regular class consumption converges,
so in those cells $C\to0$ --- \emph{collapse}. This subsection completes the classification
by characterising it: a scope distinction that organises everything, a lemma of common
necessary conditions, and the agreed lead case, Cobb--Douglas technology under exhaustible
discovery --- the cell of the classic exhaustible-resource literature, now with a recycling
loop attached. Derivations and proofs are in Appendix~\ref{sec:app:collapse}.

\begin{definition}[Collapse path]\label{def:sp:lr:collapse}
A regular solution of Problem~\ref{prob:sp} follows a \emph{collapse path} if $C\to0$.
\end{definition}

\smalltitle{Damped and catastrophic collapse.} With $\eta>1$, $u\to-\infty$ as $C\to0$, and
along an exponential decline $C\sim e^{g_Ct}$ lifetime utility is finite if and only if
\begin{align}
\left(\eta-1\right)\left|g_C\right|<\rho .
\label{eq:sp:lr:damping}
\end{align}
Call a collapse \emph{damped} when $U_0>-\infty$ and \emph{catastrophic} otherwise. The
condition~\eqref{eq:sp:lr:damping} carries far more than the value: it is simultaneously the
convergence condition of every forward price integral and the transversality condition on a
collapse path (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:collapse:scope}) --- finite value, well-defined prices
and transversality are one and the same restriction on the speed of decline. When some
feasible path is damped, no catastrophic path is optimal and the analysis below applies;
when every feasible path is catastrophic the discounted criterion assigns $-\infty$ across
the board and loses its selection power (gap~(xiv)). A second distinction decides which case
obtains: call essentiality \emph{hard} if production is impossible below a positive material
floor ($\underline R>0$) and \emph{soft} if output is positive at every $R>0$ yet cannot be
sustained as $R\to0$ (the workhorse at $\varsigma\le1$ with $\delta>0$). Under hard
essentiality every collapse is catastrophic --- the loop cannot hold $R\ge\underline R$ on
dying inflows, so production halts at a finite date
(Lemma~\ref{lem:app:col:shutdown}) --- and the damped profiles below therefore live under
soft essentiality.

\smalltitle{Collapse under trends.} Trend growth shrinks the collapse region to a rate race:
consumption dies only where the material decline outruns the trends,
$g_C=\gamma_Ag_A+g_B+g_R<0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:trend}(iii)), so every collapse
cell of the stationary map is either rescued --- by the trending BCP, by trend-paced DC, or
at the floor by the floor-anchored candidate --- or survives only with a steeper decline
behind it. The damping condition~\eqref{eq:sp:lr:damping} is untouched: it restricts the
speed of decline of $C$, whatever paces it. The profiles below (the lemma and the lead case)
are derived at stationary technology, where Appendix~\ref{sec:app:collapse} carries them in
full; their trending counterparts --- and the prior question of whether any cell optimally
collapses at a positive trend vector --- are gap~(xix). One inheritance does transfer
immediately: the shutdown lemma is mass-balance logic, so hard essentiality still turns any
collapse that does occur catastrophic, trends or not.

\begin{lemma}[Common necessary conditions on damped collapse]\label{lem:sp:lr:collapse}
Any regular damped collapse (with $\delta>0$) satisfies, as $t\to\infty$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{The depreciation floor:} $I+\delta K\ge0$ caps the speed of decline of the
    capital stock, $g_K\ge-\delta$: an economy cannot dismantle itself faster than its
    capital depreciates. Equality is the zero-gross-investment corner, on which nothing is
    replaced and consumption is net output.
    \item \emph{A below-discount interest rate:} $r\to\rho+\eta g_C<\rho$, with the capital
    margin settling at $F_Kw_Y\to\left(\rho+\eta g_C+\delta\right)\bar q>0$.
    \item \emph{The economy dies whole:} in the collapse cells, $Y,K,R,N,D,W,M^K\to0$.
    Recycling cannot avert this: the loop is a return flow, not a source --- storage gives
    back at most what investment put in, net of depreciation and loop losses --- so a
    material side without virgin inflow is strictly losing mass
    (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:collapse:lemma}, the mass-balance step).
    \item \emph{The environment recovers and is priced away:} $\Xi\to0$, $P\to0$, and the
    marginal damage in goods units dies, $p^P\to0$ for the workhorse: as consumption dies,
    marginal utility explodes and pollution becomes cheap in the only units the margins use.
    Collapse is the one configuration class that never interrogates the damage tails ---
    no analogue of the $\beta_v,\beta_F$ conditions appears.
    \item \emph{Scarcity dies in value:} $p^S\to0$ and $p^X\to0$, even where the stocks they
    price are stranded.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Collapse under the workhorse: the Cobb--Douglas lead case]
\label{prop:sp:wh:collapse}
Take the workhorse at $\varsigma=1$
($F=\left[1-\Gamma\right]b_0\big(K^Y\big)^{1-\beta}R^\beta$, $\underline R=0$, scale
$b_0>0$), $\delta>0$, $g_A=g_B=0$,
regime (E). Any regular damped collapse with exponential rates and $g_C=g_K$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Self-similar descent:} one common rate
    $g\equiv g_C=g_K=g_Y=g_R=g_W=g_{M^K}\in\left[-\delta,0\right)$, every ratio and every
    normalised price converging: a stationary-state price system on an exponentially dying
    path.
    \item \emph{The speed:} the interior $g$ solves the level system of
    Appendix~\ref{sec:app:collapse:cd}; when that system returns $g<-\delta$ the collapse
    runs at the corner instead, $g=-\delta$ with zero gross investment, which is consistent
    with the capital margin exactly when $\left(\eta-1\right)\delta<\rho$ --- the fastest
    feasible collapse is damped precisely when it is priceable. In the wedge-light benchmark
    the speed falls with the quality of what remains: a richer stranded reserve, or cheaper
    recycling, slows the descent.
    \item \emph{Stranding, not exhaustion:} $S\to\bar S>0$, pinned (in the interior variant)
    by the terminal extraction margin
    $\bar\Psi=c_N\bar S^{-\chi}\bar w_N+\bar p^W\!\left(1+\Omega^{N,S}\right)$; the Hotelling
    rent dies with the economy, $p^S\sim e^{gt}$; exploration stops at a finite date with
    $X\to\bar X<\bar X_{\max}$. The economy leaves ore in the ground and blanks on the map:
    reserves and deposits are both stranded.
    \item \emph{Circular descent:} treatment and recycling settle at interior
    stationary-like levels, and the loop is fed from two dying sources --- extraction, and
    the release of embodied material from the shrinking capital stock,
    $\bar W\left(1-\bar a\bar\varpi\right)
    =\left(1+\Omega^{N,S}\right)\bar N+\left|g\right|\bar M^K$. In the corner variant of the
    extraction margin the first source is shut and the loop runs on the release alone: the
    economy lives on its own carcass, with the circularity gap pinned by the decay rate
    (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:collapse:cd}).
    \item \emph{Pollution dies:} $P\to0$ at rate $\min\left(\left|g\right|,\theta_0\right)$
    with $p^P\to0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Four readings. First, \emph{stranding is the signature of collapse}: every sustained
configuration under regime (E) exhausts exactly --- reserves are never stranded, because a
live economy always revives the margin --- while a dying economy abandons tonnes whose
per-tonne cost outlasts their worth. ``Reserves are never stranded'' is a
sustained-consumption theorem, not a general one. Second, the capital stock changes sides:
on MEG the built environment is the landfill, absorbing every net tonne; on collapse the flow
reverses and \emph{the building stock becomes the mine}, its depreciation feeding the loop
that keeps the last production running. Third, the classic benchmark sits at the boundary:
at $\delta=0$ the lead case reproduces the declining-consumption optimum of the
exhaustible-resource literature, $g_C=-\rho/\eta$ on a run-down resource with $K$ intact;
positive depreciation changes the endgame --- capital dies too, the decline is floored at
$-\delta$, and the descent is priced off the stranded margin rather than off exhaustion.
Fourth, the environmental problem solves itself on the way down: pollution vanishes in
quantity and in price, so none of the damage-tail conditions that discipline the growth
paths has a counterpart here --- the planner's last concern is materials, not pollution.

\begin{remark}[The growth-(E)-essential cell]\label{rem:sp:lr:growthcollapse}
Past the growth threshold, collapse is not available as a regular damped limit: the CES
kernel keeps $F_K$ above its $AK$ backbone at \emph{every} point of the domain, so with
vanishing wedges $r>\rho$ and consumption cannot die (with $\phi^I=0$ this is exact;
with $\phi^I>0$ a bounded ``wedge-choked'' exception survives as a level condition,
Lemma~\ref{lem:app:col:nogrowthcollapse}). In the growth-(E) cell with essential materials
and failed matching, sustained consumption and damped collapse are therefore \emph{both}
unavailable: the cell holds no damped regular path at all --- the (E)-column twin of
gap~(xii). An economy in that cell grows into a wall: hard essentiality makes every collapse
catastrophic, and what the optimum does --- a non-regular descent, or a ranking of
catastrophic paths by an overtaking criterion --- is open (gap~(xiv)). Trend growth offers
this cell its one exit: the floor-anchored closed-loop BCP of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sp:lr:trend}(iii), a rate-consistent candidate whenever
$\left(g_A,g_B\right)\ne\left(0,0\right)$; whether it exists as an optimum is part of
gap~(xviii).
\end{remark}
